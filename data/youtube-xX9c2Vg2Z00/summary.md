# Andy Burnham's remarks: first PMQs exchange with Kemi Badenoch

Source: [PoliticsJOE — Andy Burnham and Kemi Badenoch face off in first ever PMQs clash](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xX9c2Vg2Z00).

YouTube displays a publication date of 2 September 2026. Notes prepared on 24 October 2026 from the video's displayed transcript.

This is a paraphrased, timestamped record of Burnham's responses, rather than a verbatim transcript. Statements below are attributed to Burnham; their factual accuracy has not been independently checked. Badenoch's questions are included only as context.

## 00:35–01:28 — Welfare and support for young people

[Watch this response](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xX9c2Vg2Z00&t=35s).

Asked whether he had refused any requests for additional spending, Burnham says his earlier statement made reducing welfare expenditure a clear objective. He regards the number of people in their twenties outside work, education and training as unacceptable. His proposed approach is to give young people more support so they can succeed, reducing the longer-term cost of unsuccessful outcomes. He invites Badenoch to cooperate on practical solutions.

## 02:06–03:21 — Tax reductions and spending priorities

[Watch this response](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xX9c2Vg2Z00&t=126s).

Asked what he has cut, Burnham points to tax reductions. He says he announced the removal of VAT on electricity bills shortly after taking office, followed by reduced business rates for pubs, social clubs and live music venues, with the chancellor's support. He presents these measures as help with household and business costs.

He says the commitments are fully funded and that money was redirected from digital ID, which he does not regard as a government priority.

## 04:10–05:32 — Growth, debt and Jim O'Neill

[Watch this response](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xX9c2Vg2Z00&t=250s).

Responding to criticism attributed to Jim O'Neill and a question about borrowing costs, Burnham describes their ten-year working relationship. He says O'Neill offers frank advice and that they sometimes disagree. He credits their collaboration with Greater Manchester's economic progress.

He criticizes the Conservatives' record on growth and the debt-to-GDP ratio, arguing that it left the country vulnerable to market turbulence. He claims that, during the first half of the year, the UK achieved the G7's strongest growth, borrowing reached a six-year low, and deficit reduction outpaced other G7 countries.

## 05:57–07:05 — Budget timing and the Truss government

[Watch this response](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xX9c2Vg2Z00&t=357s).

Asked whether further tax increases are planned, Burnham repeats that his initial measures reduced taxes. He says the budget was scheduled early to limit speculation.

He then criticizes Badenoch's shadow cabinet appointments, linking several members to Liz Truss's 49-day government. He attributes damage to economic confidence and mortgage costs to that administration. He contrasts the outgoing shadow chancellor's criticism of the mini-budget with the replacement's alleged involvement in producing it. He does not explicitly rule out future tax increases in this response.

## Approximately 08:14–09:23 — Borrowing and fiscal rules

[Watch this response](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xX9c2Vg2Z00&t=494s).

Burnham returns to the previous government's debt record and challenges the characterization of the new shadow chancellor's role during the Truss budget. He again links that budget to increased mortgage costs.

He commits to reducing debt, observing the fiscal rules and easing pressure on household budgets. He does not explicitly rule out additional borrowing.

## Approximately 10:34–12:15 — Russia, defence and national growth

[Watch this response](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xX9c2Vg2Z00&t=634s).

The transcript resumes partway through Burnham's response. He condemns an attack in Leipzig attributed to Russia and expresses solidarity with Germany and Ukraine.

He says the chancellor's commitment to defence helped determine his appointment. He promises full funding for the defence investment plan and says the spending review will explain how the government intends to meet its NATO commitments by 2035.

He concludes by promoting a national growth programme informed by his experience in Greater Manchester, aiming to spread economic opportunity and restore public optimism.
